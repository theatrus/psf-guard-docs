Calibration Libraries

Catalog bias, dark, dark-flat and flat frames beside the lights they serve. PSF Guard grades each frame, measures how a rig's flats change from night to night, matches conservatively, and builds masters when a stack needs them. The raw frames stay where they are.

Calibrate
Add calibration folders to the catalog that holds your lights.

Import reads headers only. Grading and flat measuring follow in the background; stacks, WBPP runs and exports then take the matching frames.

Add frames ↓
New after 0.11. The Calibration page replaces the calibration library dialog. Frame grades, flat groups, dust marks, calibration health and settings for one rig are new too.

Add calibration frames

  1. In Settings › Databases, add the folders that hold the calibration files to the catalog's image directories.
  2. Run Import. Choose Calibration only to skip lights, and check the dry preview before applying it. Creating a database and the CLI use the same header-only scan, and automatic import can take new calibration frames on a schedule; see Import Images.
  3. Click Open Calibration on the database's calibration summary, or the header's Calibration tab, to see what arrived.

PSF Guard sorts frames by their IMAGETYP header:

Library typeHeader values
BiasBIAS, OFFSET
DarkDARK
Dark flatDARK FLAT, DARKFLAT, FLATDARK
FlatFLAT, TWILIGHT FLAT

Twilight flats stay in the calibration library during folder imports and remote uploads. They never become light rows or exposure templates that could sync back to Target Scheduler. Masters from other software import too; see below.

Calibration records live in PSF Guard's own tables inside the catalog. Pixels do not: records point at the raw files, and generated masters live in the calibration masters folder. Reading an untouched Target Scheduler database creates no tables, and a dry run rolls its changes back.

Rigs

A rig is the telescope, camera, sensor size and binning the headers record. N.I.N.A. profile IDs are recorded beside it, so a copied database carries its calibration library without tying it to one machine. Header text counts as written: a renamed telescope, or a stray space in TELESCOP or INSTRUME, makes another rig.

The Calibration page

The header's Calibration tab, between Sequence and Stacks, shows the library of the database in the header's scope: its sessions, each frame drawn as a picture, one frame's numbers beside its session's, and the masters built from them. Here the header's Rig · target control names the rig only. Other ways in:

  • Open Calibration on a database's calibration summary in Settings › Databases;
  • Browse calibration in a project's coverage report;
  • a night in an image's Calibration panel, which the 🧪 chip in Images and Sequence opens;
  • Open in Calibration on a master in a stack's Masters inspector.

The page has three tabs: Frames, Masters, and Scheduler flats for Target Scheduler's flat coverage (see N.I.N.A. Sync Plugin). The address keeps every choice, from the filters to the frame and its stretch, so a reload or a shared link opens the same view. Viewers see the frames; editors also get the buttons that change them.

One project's calibration

Opened from a project's Images, Sequence or Stacks, or from its coverage report, the page shows only what that project's lights use: the sessions their masters come from, those masters, and the flat groups they touch, on every rig the project was shot with. A chip such as M 44 on Askar 120 ✕ heads the filter bar. Its ✕, or Show all of Askar 120's calibration under the heading, widens to the rig's whole library. Choosing another project in the header moves the scope to it. Opened from the Library or the Sky, the page shows the whole library.

Sessions and frames

The list runs newest night first, each session with its frames counted by grade. A chosen session shows its frames as a strip of small pictures, in the order shot unless Order says otherwise, with one in the viewer. Sessions | Groups, at the top of the sidebar, lists flat groups instead.

One frame

The panel beside the viewer gives the frame's file, capture time and settings, its grade and main reason, and each number grading measured beside the median of its session: for a flat its level, clipped share, noise and spots, such as 15 spots · 3 large disks; for a bias or dark its level and spread in ADU, noise, row and column banding and hot pixels. Below them sit the limits the frame crossed, each with its value and the limit. An editor gets Keep out, Use anyway and Clear, the validity marks and Forget.

Forget removes the frame's catalog record, every master built from it, and every master built on those. The FITS file stays. The session's header has Forget session and the validity marks for every frame of the session.

The Masters tab

Masters lists every master the library recorded, newest first: its kind, filter or exposure, its source frames with their nights and temperatures, when it was built, and the projects whose latest stacks use it. Badges mark a bias or dark master of fewer than five frames (Thin), a flat master whose set carries changing spots (Poor · spots or Suspect · spots), one the flat check Set aside, one from Other software, and a Missing file. View opens the master in the inspector a stack's Masters button opens, with a display stretch, zoom and an unchanged FITS download, and no stack needed. Clear generated masters, under ⋯, removes every generated master and keeps every raw frame and record.

Nights

A night runs from local noon where a frame was shot to the next local noon, as N.I.N.A.'s DATEMINUS12 folders date it. Dusk flats, the night's darks and the next dawn's flats share one date, and a dawn flat run never splits in two. Sessions, grades, flat groups, the coverage report and export folders all use these nights. Noon comes from the first of these that a frame has:

  1. its rig's Rig's time zone setting, a UTC offset (Automatic (from frames) by default);
  2. the site longitude in its header (SITELONG, the N.I.N.A. profile's site);
  3. its computer's clock (DATE-LOC less DATE-OBS);
  4. where its rig usually is;
  5. the daytime hours when the catalog's lights are never captured.

A rig taken to a star party keeps its home nights. Set Rig's time zone for a rig whose frames record no site and whose clock is wrong too, such as a remote rig kept on UTC. After the upgrade, Reading frame sites in the header's job line reads each older frame's site once, with Stop, and tries again while the server runs if the NAS is not mounted. PSF Guard then grades and groups those rigs again, and measures only the flat runs that were split.

Capture times on the Calibration page read on the rig's clock with its offset, such as 2026-08-11 06:23 UTC−7; when the server built a master reads on your clock. Images and Sequence split lights at each rig's noon too. Nights end, in Settings › Calibration, puts them back on one split for the whole catalog with Same time for all rigs.

Frame grades

PSF Guard grades every raw bias, dark, dark flat and flat against the frames that should agree with it. Masters from other software get no grade.

Darks that caught stray light

A dark shot while light reached the sensor, such as dawn through an open roof or a cap left off, has the right headers. Its pixels sit above the bias, often more with each frame. A master from such darks pushes every light's sky below zero and carves a bowl that later shows as colored corners.

A dark more than one noise sigma above the quietest frame that could stand in for it, from any night, grades Poor · stray light, so a morning whose darks all leaked is still caught by a clean set from another night. A lone frame with nothing to compare against stays in use. The frame panel gives each dark's level and spread in ADU beside its session's, and Use anyway puts such a dark back into its master.

Dust in flats

Grading finds the shadows in each flat. A spot is a small patch that passes less light than the response around it. Dust further up the train casts large, faint shadows: out-of-focus disks, such as a RedCat61's 600-pixel disks under half a percent deep, and donuts, dust seen through an SCT's central obstruction. These count apart, as large dust: 15 spots · 3 large disks. A spot of a few pixels must clear a threshold raised for the size of the frame, so a large sensor does not count its own noise.

Spots, above the viewer, draws what grading found at its shape: a spot as its box, a disk as its rim, a donut as its ring and hole. Large dust, nested under Spots, turns the disks, donuts and vignetting on and off. Vignetting, a shadow hanging from an edge or corner such as an off-axis guider's prism, draws dashed and never counts as dust. Each large mark gives its depth and, when the header gives the focal ratio and pixel size, how far in front of the sensor the dust sits: disk 0.33% · ~11 mm, filter/window. Hover or tap a mark for its numbers.

With Compare with session on, the marks show the session's own dust, found on the stack of its flats, with the number of flats each shows in. What one flat alone holds, such as a drop of dew drying, draws as a transient. Only shadows count: a star in a sky flat adds light, so it never counts as a spot or as dew.

Flat groups

Dust moves, arrives and leaves between nights. A light divided by flats from the wrong side of such a change carries the difference as a faint ghost. PSF Guard measures how alike each night's flats are, keeps nights that differ out of one master, and gives each light flats from its own side of each change. The full rules are in docs/CALIBRATION_LIBRARY.md.

Measuring flats

PSF Guard combines the flats of each session of two or more into one response, and compares two responses as later ÷ earlier. That gives two numbers, in percent of the sky background a light would be left with: dust, the deepest shadow that arrived, left or moved, and light pattern, the largest broad difference, such as a changed vignette, after a straight gradient is taken out. Dust that moved no further than focus travel explains, as an SCT's donuts do, draws but does not count. A guider's corner shadow that turns with the camera counts as vignetting, held to the light-pattern limit, not as new dust.

Measuring flats is a job of its own in the header's job line, with the rig, 12 of 40 sessions and Stop. It measures only the sessions an import, sync, upload, forget or duplicate repair touched, so an up-to-date catalog reads no pixel. The rig on screen in Frames goes first. Stop pauses measuring until those flats change, Resume now, or a restart. A changed limit regroups at once from the stored numbers.

Angles and response families

A rotator angle in the header is a guide, not proof of the light path. Within one rig, filter, focal length and sensor mode, angles whose gaps stay within the rig's rotation tolerance (at most 2°) count as one, so a rotator that wanders by a degree or two still compares its flats. Angles whose flats measure alike form one response family, named in Frames by its measured angle range: 100.1–104.4°, or 72.6° & 82.2°. Flats shot through a reducer are never compared with flats shot without it. Flats that record no angle join the one family they measure alike with, or else serve a light only when no flat of its angle does.

Groups, splits and changes

Within a family, nights join into groups in time order while their dust and light pattern stay within the limits, 1% and 2% by default. Every night of a group measured alike with the others, so a slow drift cannot chain unlike nights into one master. Between two groups lies a split. Splits that overlap in time, with shadows at the same place on the sensor, form one change. A change seen in two filters, or in two families of one filter, is on the camera side and applies to every light of the rig; one seen in one filter applies to that filter only.

Which flats a light takes

  1. Its side of each change: by its time when shot outside the change's window, else by its imaging session, else by its own pixels fitted against the change's map. A light none of these places is undetermined.
  2. Its family: the one at its recorded angle. Where two families lie within 10°, as after a rotator re-homed, its own pixels choose, and move it off its recorded angle only when its rotator's epochs or another filter's lights agree.
  3. Its flats: its family's flats at its own angle, from its side of every change. Another angle's flats serve only where they measured alike on several nights, or its pixels chose the family. With none on its side, it takes the nearest other angle's flats when the rig's flats barely depend on the angle, else its own angle's flats corrected across a camera-side change, else its own angle's flats from the other side, flagged.

A light's Calibration panel says how its flats were chosen, under Chosen by, and names its Flat family when that was not the one at its recorded angle. Flats from across a change raises a Check on a light that had to take flats from the other side, or whose side nothing tells. Nights measured alike mix in one master with no Check.

Flat groups in Frames

Whenever the list can hold flats (Type All or Flat):

  • Each flat session carries its group, such as Group 3 · 09-07 → 09-16, 4 nights, with a coloured band down its edge.
  • A split is a line above the newest session of the older group, with its reason: the light pattern changed by 2.6% (limit 2%). A camera-side change shows once, with its depth, the filters it was seen in, and its window on the rig's clock.
  • Compare, on a split or a change, shows the later flats divided by the earlier, at ±3%, ±1% or ±0.5%, with new and departed shadows circled.
  • A line above the list gives each rig's limits and unmeasured sessions, and ends in Edit limits, which opens Settings › Calibration › Flat groups on the rig's own value or the global one.

Groups, under Sessions | Groups, lists the groups themselves, newest first by rig, filter and angle (such as G · 3.2°), with the split between neighbours and its reason in between: Change · new dust 1.6%, Split · pattern 2.6%. Marks name the Current group, which a light shot now takes, one Partly measured, and one with a Change after. ↑ and ↓ move between groups. A group shows its flats in the strip night by night, then Members (its nights), Why they belong together (how far apart they measured, against the limits), Its edges (each side's split or change, with Compare) and What it feeds: its flat masters, and the lights that take it by project, each opening Images on exactly those lights. In a project scope, the groups either side of the project's own show dimmed as Neighbour.

Choose by hand

Where the measurements are wrong, a choice made by hand wins:

  • Split here, on a night under Members, starts a new group at that night. Lights take flats from their side of it by time.
  • Join, on the edge between two groups in the list, or Join with next and Join with previous under Its edges, makes two groups one, without the mixed-flats Check. A join across a camera-side change says so; the change still decides which side that filter's lights take.
  • A light's Calibration panel, under Choose its flats, offers Before and After for each change it was shot during, and Use group for one group's flats. Every light of its imaging session applies the choice to the whole run.
  • Flat side…, in Images, sets Before, After or Reset on the selected lights, for each change they were shot during.

Edges chosen by hand read Split by you or Joined by you, with the measured numbers beside them and Reset. A choice whose flats or change are gone stays, shown as no longer applied, until reset. Choices travel with Merge catalogs and sync pull. Changing them needs write access and database management: the desktop app, or a server started with --allow-database-management.

A light's Calibration panel

An image's detail view has a Calibration panel. It lists the light's findings in words, worst first, and for each kind the frames its master would take, by night and sensor temperature, with the frames Kept out, those that Also match, not used, and those Refused, each with why. Each night opens its frames on the Calibration page. Choosing a finding picks out the frames behind it, such as the two nights a flat master mixes, and stays chosen from image to image; Show every kind shows the rest again.

The panel names whose settings checked the light (Checked by the global settings, or by the rig's own), each limit that differs from its default and who set it, and under Not flagged what a setting kept from being flagged. A refusal names its limit: dark is 3.2 °C warmer than the light (limit 2 °C, set for Askar 120 · ASI2600MM). Tune checks for the rig opens its settings. For flats the panel adds Flat family, Chosen by and Choose its flats. It opens by itself on a Poor or Check light, and when you come from the 🧪 chip.

Check coverage for a project

Click Calibration on a project's full card in the Library, or its 🧪 pill. The report uses the same matcher as a stack, so it shows what would apply, not just what files exist. Browse calibration, at its top, opens the Calibration page on that project.

  • Quality lists each health finding with its lights by night and filter, and opens those lights in Images.
  • Per kind: how many frames match, how many sessions they span, and the newest.
  • Per night and filter: the flat set a master would use, how far it is from the lights, and whether the night has its own flats. The dark column shows what one dark master would use; hover for every matching dark within reach.
  • A night with no flats shows the nearest flat the match refused only for its rotator angle, how far off it is, and the limit: the usual cause when a rotator moved.
  • Each night lists the masters from other software that fit its lights: used, matching but not used, or not used with the readings that disagree, such as rotation light=92.6deg master=94.7deg.
  • A night and filter whose lights can't be calibrated says why, and opens that night's lights in Images.

Warnings list kinds with no matches, nights without same-night flats, and flats more than a month from their lights. The report leaves rejected lights out, so a night rejected for cloud does not show as lacking flats; Include N rejected lights adds them back.

Lights that can't be calibrated

A light can't be calibrated when a check set to flag Poor finds a gap: no flat, though the library holds flats for its camera, as when every flat for its filter was shot at another rotator angle; no bias or dark, though the library holds either; or flats further away than Flat age limit (60 days; 0 lifts it). Each rule counts only where the library could have filled the gap, so a rig that never shoots flats is not affected. A check set to Check only or Off never makes a light one that can't be calibrated.

Check lights, at the foot of the report, lists the project's lights that can't be calibrated, by night, filter and reason. Reject N lights then rejects them with that reason, so Target Scheduler shoots them again. If the lights changed since the check, it refuses and asks for a new check. Rejecting needs write access; agents have the same check over MCP.

Stacks keep such lights unless Leave out lights that can't be calibrated, under Settings › Calibration › Stacks and exports, is on. It is off by default. With it on, and calibration on Auto or Force on, stacks leave those lights out: the card counts them under can't calibrate, and Frame decisions gives each one's reason. Every export leaves out the same lights.

Safe matching

A calibration frame must agree with every setting the light records. If the light records a value and the frame does not, they do not match. A frame with no known camera name or sensor size never matches.

MasterMust agree
BiasCamera, dimensions, channels, binning, gain, offset, readout mode, Bayer layout
DarkBias fields; exposure within the larger of 0.1% or 0.05 s; temperature within Dark temperature (3 °C); shot within Dark reach of the light
Dark flatThe flat's sensor settings, exposure and temperature
FlatBias fields, filter, telescope, focal length within Flat focal length (1 mm), and rotator angle within Flat rotation (2°) when both record it

The readout mode counts however the driver spelled it. N.I.N.A. writes a name such as Extend Fullwell 2CMS; PSF Guard compares names ignoring case, so a High Gain dark never serves an Extend Fullwell light.

Frames in one master must also agree with each other: temperatures within 1 °C, and flats within 2°, 1 mm and one Flat session of each other. With flats grouped by measurement, only nights that measured alike pool. The nearest group with enough frames builds the master, up to 64 frames. Before integrating, Seiza checks the inputs again and leaves out any it will not accept, such as a frame filed under the wrong filter, camera or readout mode; the stack warning lists them.

Mark where calibration stops being valid

Nearest in time is the right default, but it cannot know about a dust cleaning or a re-spaced imaging train. Flat groups catch many such changes in flats; mark the rest by hand. On the Calibration page, the session's header marks every frame of a session, and the frame panel one frame: ◂ Before only for the last set before the change, After only ▸ for a set shot right after it, and Both to clear the mark. Matching never takes a marked frame across its boundary, for any kind. A light shot at the boundary instant matches either way, and a frame with no capture time keeps matching. Marks survive rescans and sync with the library.

Dark masters

Darks keep for months, so a dark master can draw on several nights. It uses the nearest complete night of darks alone. When no night is complete, it pools the nights within reach, nearest first. Three settings under Settings › Calibration › Dark masters shape this, for every rig or one:

SettingDefaultEffect
Dark reach183 daysA dark shot further than this from the light does not match. A dark marked to serve lights before or after it follows its mark instead, however old.
Complete night10 darksThe nearest night with this many matching darks makes the master alone. With fewer, nights pool.
Dark choiceComplete set firstWhich set of darks, or bias, builds the master when several match.

A dark matches a light within 3 °C, but one master takes only darks within 1 °C of each other, so several sets can match. With Complete set first, the nearest set with at least Complete night frames builds the master, and a nearer, smaller set serves only when no set is complete. Nearest set keeps the old rule, the nearest two or more frames however few, and a two-frame master puts its own read noise into every light as row banding. Bias masters follow the same choice, and WBPP runs and exports take the same frames. A rig with no matching darks can turn Use darks off: its lights take bias and flats, with no missing-dark flag.

Masters from other software

A master bias, dark or flat from WBPP, Siril or another tool imports like any calibration frame. A master IMAGETYP or an NCOMBINE card marks it, and the frame panel shows a Master badge. PSF Guard uses it as-is, never integrated again, and does not grade it.

  • Matching is lenient, since such files often drop gain, offset and temperature: what both sides record must agree, and the camera name or sensor size must still match. A dark's exposure must still match. The stack report shows what the match took on trust.
  • A master dark that records no sensor temperature, such as one from WBPP, calibrates cooled lights.
  • WBPP's FITS masters are floats scaled 0 to 1. PSF Guard copies such a master into its cache once, on the same 16-bit scale as every other frame. The original is never written to.

External masters, under Settings › Calibration › Matching, decides how they take part, for every rig or one: Whenever one matches (default), Only if raw frames can't build one, or Never.

Calibration in stacks

The Calibration control in Stacks has three modes:

ModeWhat it does
AutoDefault. Applies only masters whose matching and dependencies are safe.
Force onApplies every master that can be built, including a flat with no trusted pedestal. The card warns about the risk.
OffSkips matching and stacks the raw lights.

The project-wide choice is saved, and each channel card can override it. A stack whose lights need different flats or darks splits into calibration sessions and swaps masters between them; the card reports the session count. Calibration runs on raw CFA data before debayering. To keep lights the library can't calibrate out of stacks, see above.

A master that fails to build does not fail the stack. The lights stack without it, and the card shows the skipped master and why. A master whose input master failed, such as a flat whose bias did not build, is skipped rather than built without it.

Flats

A flat divides correctly only after the light's pedestal is removed, which a bias or dark master does. With flats only, PSF Guard fits the pedestal from the lights when the field has enough vignetting and the fit passes its checks; otherwise Auto leaves the flat out and says to import bias or dark frames.

A flat master gets a defect pass after integration, since a hot or dead pixel repeats in every flat and would print into every light. Darks keep their hot pixels, which subtract the lights' own; lights with no dark master get the same pixel filter before stacking.

Let stars move between sky flats. Dither or let the sky drift. A star that stays on the same pixels survives clipping, and star images are too wide for the defect pass to remove.

Mask stars in flats, under Settings › Calibration › Flat masters, detects stars in each raw flat and leaves them out before integration. It is off by default, applies to every rig, and does not touch imported masters. Every pixel must keep at least two unmasked frames, or the flat build fails and the stack goes on without it.

Dew drying off the sensor window leaves spots that fade from one flat to the next. PSF Guard reads each new flat master back against its frames, counting only dips, so stars in sky flats do not count. When a minority disagree, the master rebuilds without them; when the whole set disagrees with itself, the next-nearest flat set takes its place; with no other set, the master serves and the card says to retake it.

Masters

Each master needs at least two frames. Bias and dark masters use a two-pass, leave-one-out sigma-clipped mean; flat masters use median/MAD clipping after calibrating and normalizing each frame. The FITS records the method, thresholds, frame count and rejected samples. A master builds on the cores its stack build was given; see Processor use. While it builds, the stack card and the header queue show which master and the frame or tile under way, and Stop ends the build and writes nothing.

Each master's file name comes from its input frames, file fingerprints, upstream masters and algorithm versions, so a changed bias rebuilds the dark and flat masters that used it, and an unchanged master is reused. Masters live in the calibration masters folder, which is the cache unless --calibration-dir or Settings › Storage › Folders sets another:

<calibration masters folder>/<database>/calibration-masters/

PSF Guard removes old and half-written masters once unused, keeps any master whose frames are gone, and builds a removed master again when a stack needs it. See Disk use.

Masters on a finished stack shows the files that stack used, with the sensor temperatures and nights of their frames, so a dark master shot warmer than its lights stands out. Open in Calibration opens a master on the Calibration page's Masters tab.

Duplicates

Capture software drops frames where it likes, and a filer later copies them into the calibration tree and deletes the originals. A frame on disk twice is catalogued once: when the original goes, its row moves to the copy and keeps its grade, marks and masters. Two files count as one exposure only when kind, capture time to the second, file name, rig, capture settings and file size agree, and, with both on disk, their first and last 32 KiB match.

An older import may have recorded one exposure twice, and a master built from both may have averaged it twice. To merge them:

  1. Tick Duplicates among the Calibration page's filters. The view lists each exposure recorded more than once, which of its files are Missing, which row a repair keeps, and when it was checked. Check again checks at once. Any account may check.
  2. Click Repair duplicates…. The preview says how many rows merge and which masters build again, across every rig and type whatever the filters show.
  3. Click Apply. Each exposure keeps its oldest row, moved to a live copy when its own file is gone, with the grade, newest choice and validity mark carried over. Masters built from merged rows build again.

The check and the repair run as jobs, on the page and in the header's job line (Checking files 812 of 2,140, then Merging 37 of 203), with Stop. A stopped repair writes nothing. Apply checks the files again and, if the plan changed, shows the new plan instead. The repair moves and deletes no file, and a later pull from a catalog that still has the duplicates does not bring them back. Repairing needs database management: the desktop app, or a server started with --allow-database-management.

From the command line

# Preview the duplicate repair, then apply it
psf-guard calibration repair-duplicates scheduler.sqlite --dry-run
psf-guard calibration repair-duplicates scheduler.sqlite

# Measure flat groups on a copy; the catalog is only read
psf-guard flat-groups scheduler.sqlite --work ./flat-work --lights --report flat-groups.json

repair-duplicates holds a lock beside the catalog, so a server and the command line never repair one catalog at once. On a dry run against a catalog from another machine, --map-prefix /mnt/share=/Volumes/share reads its paths through this machine's mount. flat-groups copies the catalog into --work, measures there, and prints the groups, splits and changes; --images finds the lights for pixel evidence, --lights says how each light's flats would be chosen, and --report writes JSON. It never writes the catalog or a frame. See CLI Reference.

Export with calibration frames

Project and target exports add, for each light, the raw frames a stack would build its masters from, matched by the settings for the light's rig. Poor frames, frames kept out and darks that caught stray light stay out.

  • Flats go under their target and night, placed by flat group as a stack takes them, and lights of one night that need different flats get a session each. WBPP cannot carry flats across a change, so such a light keeps its flats as shot and is named in the export summary and the runner.
  • When lights of one exposure were matched to different darks, each set of darks goes in its own DARKSET_<night> folder, so WBPP does not average every night's darks into one master.
  • With Leave out lights that can't be calibrated on, every export leaves out the lights a stack would, in either layout, from the dialog, the CLI and the AstroBin export, and says how many and why.

Layouts, options and the WBPP runner are on Export & AstroBin.

Calibrated copies

Calibrated and registered copies that WBPP, Siril, ASTAP, DeepSkyStacker and other tools write are paired with the light each came from, instead of importing as extra lights. A calibrated copy with no raw frame becomes the light until the raw arrives. Stacks still read the raw frames and calibrate them from this library. See Import Images.

Move a catalog

Merge catalogs and sync pull copy rigs, profile bindings, raw calibration records with their grades and validity marks, Keep out and Use anyway choices, flat groups split or joined by hand, lights put on a side of a change, and duplicate repairs. Where both catalogs hold a choice, the later one wins. The transfer preview counts the choices a pull brings, since each changes the destination's masters. They do not copy generated masters, whose paths belong to the source machine. The destination finds stale raw paths by file name under its own image folders and builds its own masters.

PSF Guard 0.11 keeps working on a catalog this version has graded. It leaves out the darks graded for stray light and ignores the other grades and choices.
